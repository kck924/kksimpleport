Total Request Live:
A Decade of Data

For nearly ten years, adolescent millennials came home from school, threw their backpacks on the ground, and crowded the television to watch MTV count down the 10 most-requested videos from an iconic studio above Times Square. Now we can revisit those days with the implements of adulthood: charts and data.

By Kevin Klein · Data from fan recaps archived by the Wayback Machine
scroll to play it ↓

From September 1998 through October 2007, MTV’s Total Request Live broadcast nearly 2,000 top-10 countdowns, featuring 975 different music videos, 240 of which spent time in the coveted #1 spot. Use the below tool to search for any song or artist to see when and where they spent time on the countdown.

Hover a square to follow one video. Click it to zoom in on that week.Tap a square to zoom in on that week.

Why the wall stops in October 2007. On October 22, 2007, MTV stopped ranking the countdown on viewer requests alone and started mixing in sales, radio play and online streams. The requests became just one part of the equation. That was a fundamental shift in what the show was, and the beginning of the end: TRL aired its final countdown a year later, in November 2008. So the wall ends on October 18, 2007, the last countdown ranked purely by request.

    About the data

    Where this came from

    Countdowns are from the fan-run TRL Archive at ATRL, read from 112 monthly pages saved by the Wayback Machine. The #1s were checked against Wikipedia's list of TRL number ones and match on 97.6% of the days both cover.

    What's left out

    Weekend shows and specials such as year-end countdowns. The week of September 11, 2001, when there was no show. And everything from October 22, 2007 on, when MTV stopped ranking the countdown on requests alone (see the note under the wall).

    Judgment calls

    Genres are my own calls, made per artist, so Avril Lavigne counts as pop-punk and Rihanna as pop. Fans spelled things many ways ('N Sync, *NSYNC, Nsync); those were merged into one name per video.
