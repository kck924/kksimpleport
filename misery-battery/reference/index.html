How miserable is your battery?

Pick one team from each of MLB, NBA, NHL and the NFL and a span of seasons. The score measures how far your four teams fell short of a perfect season, averaged over every season in the span. Higher means more miserable. It is compared against every possible four-team combination over the same span.

Every combination a fan could plausibly hold, built from each team city with a 60-mile radius. When no team in a league was that close, the fan follows the nearest one. Before a local team existed, or after it left, the fan follows whichever team was local that season.

How it's scored

Each season earns points: the team's regular-season standing in its league that year (0 for last, 1 for first, so 162-game and 17-game seasons compare fairly), plus bonuses for making the playoffs, reaching the final four (LCS, conference finals or conference championship), reaching the final and winning it. A season's misery is how far it fell short of a perfect season. A team's score is the average over the seasons it played in the span, and a battery's score is the average of its four teams. The Heartbreak preset also penalizes losing in the final.

Teams that played fewer than half the seasons in the span (expansion teams, mostly) still get a score but no league rank, and they are left out of the all-combinations comparison. The NHL lockout season (2004-05) is skipped. MLB 1994 counts as a season with no playoffs for anyone. NBA play-in losers count as missing the playoffs.

Data covers 1991 to 2025 (NBA and NHL seasons 1991-92 to 2025-26). MLB from Retrosheet game logs; NFL from nflverse and FiveThirtyEight game files; NBA and NHL from Basketball-Reference and Hockey-Reference franchise histories.
